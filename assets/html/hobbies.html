Quantum Computing

This area of nascent technology appears unapproachable, the easiest way to visualize this would be rotating a circle, sphere, or hypersphere (ball in 4+ dimensions) in such a way that its north pole is pointing towards the correct answer. Each qubit, in this case, would keep track of the rotation of data in this space. The YouTube channel 3Blue1Brown has an excellent brief explainer here.

While the list of widely-available algorithms for quantum computers are somewhat limited, I was able to teach myself to a reasonably-fine-grain level with Quantum Computing: An Applied Approach by Jack D. Hidary and Programming Quantum Computers: Essential Algorithms and Code Samples by Eric R. Johnston, Nic Harrigan, and Mercedes Gimeno-Segovia.

Dungeons and Dragons

I started playing the 5th edition of this tabletop game in 2023 to experience a classic game and join the nerd culture in Austin, TX, and haven't looked back! Not only is it a near-infinite source of potential entertainment, but has lead to new friendships.

For those looking for new resources to get started in the Austin area, I would recommend Tribe Comics and Games. With regular game nights for both D&D and Warhammer, it is a great entrypoint for both hobbies in the Austin area. Regardless of whether you have ten minutes or ten years of experience, it is a welcoming environment to enjoy a strong communal hobby.

History

Why

I have two main reasons that drew me into history: academic interest and personal curiosity.

"History doesn’t repeat itself, but it does rhyme." - Mark Twain

Academically, it is interesting to know how historical events decades or centuries ago still have ripples impacting the world today. While history isn't a perfect representation of current events, it does allow us to figure out trends we should expect to see. Many would think that concerns around automation negatively impacting the labor market is a uniquely 21st century problem, but the Luddites had similar concerns around automated looms. The Luddite movement actually was the origin of the word sabotage.

My curiosity was then intrigued by people and events that tend to buck the historical trends that we would expect to see. While understanding trends is good to understand what to expect going forward, it is also healthy to be curious about when those trends do not hold. These unusual events could have a number of potential components making them more complicated, like:

  • Supernatural, like the Casket girls, immigrant girls who could be the first "vampires" in the United States. While most serious historians don't genuinely believe that New Orleans imported vampires from Europe, it is interesting to see how the supernatural collides with verifiable history.
  • Comical, like when trading onion futures becoming illegal due to the "Onion King".
  • Ironic, like the U.S. Consumer Product Safety Commission accidentally making an extremely dangerous lapel pin in 1974. Apparently, in addition to being sharp, it also contained lead paint and was a choking hazard for children. It would appear that one dangerous design decision was not ironic enough.
  • Seemingly-anachronistic, like how suitcases got wheels AFTER humanity landed on the moon.
